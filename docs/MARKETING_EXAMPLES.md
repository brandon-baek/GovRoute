# Govroute campaign concepts

These are proposed creative examples, not launched campaigns, testimonials, or
measured performance claims. They describe current UI capabilities and preserve
the distinction between a reviewed route, an official handoff, and a coverage gap.
La Crescenta is a regression case, not an endorsement or featured market.

## 1. One move. Two states. A clearer checklist.

**Format:** short motion ad or three-frame social carousel.

1. Two browser windows: “Leaving New York” and “Arriving in Texas.” Caption:
   “Moving is one life event. The paperwork doesn’t always treat it that way.”
2. The windows become one checklist. Departure and arrival labels stay visible;
   prerequisite steps appear before dependent tasks.
3. “One move. Both sides of the checklist.” CTA: **Build your moving checklist.**

Destination: `/moving`. Show the vehicle toggle and the New York-to-Texas route.
Keep a readable end card: “Checked guidance is limited to supported routes.
Confirm current requirements at official sources.” Do not imply every state's
license and registration rules are fully covered.

## 2. Your town name isn't the whole story.

**Format:** interactive product demo or an annotated split-screen ad.

Left: “I live here. Which office handles this?” Right: geographic context,
the task coverage label, and the sources needed to confirm scope.

Headline: **A place name is only the beginning.**

Body: “Start with your task. Check the relevant area. See a sourced route where
one is reviewed—and a clear gap where more evidence is needed.”

CTA: **Explore sources & coverage.** Destination: `/data#coverage`.
Show a neutral selection or rotate the same comparable cohort used by tests;
do not hard-code a featured community. Do not draw parcel boundaries, advertise
a zoning verdict, or equate geographic containment with legal authority.

## 3. Show me the next step. Show me the source.

**Format:** minimalist poster, search ad, or a 15-second screen recording.

Headline: **Your next step has a source.**

Body: “A government pathway with ordered steps, official links, visible review
status, and a checklist you can print.”

Visual: reveal a passport guide from question → ordered steps → source links →
browser-local progress. Keep the source date and any review warning visible.

CTA: **Find your pathway.** Destination: `/` or the passport guide.
Avoid “guaranteed approval,” “always up to date,” “all government services,” or
any suggestion of government affiliation.

## Launch discipline

- Keep “Independent public-service guide” visible in public-facing creative.
- Measure useful actions such as an official-source click or a printed checklist;
  do not treat an ad click as a completed government transaction.
- Compare concepts with the same audience and placement before drawing conclusions.
  There are no performance results yet.
- Do not capture street addresses or government identifiers in campaign analytics.
- Recheck the demonstrated route's source review and coverage before running an ad.
